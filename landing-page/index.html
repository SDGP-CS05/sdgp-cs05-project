Smart grow chamber for Sri Lanka

Grow oyster mushrooms without watching the thermometer.

A 1.7 m cabinet for 15 sawdust bags. It sprays every bag through its own nozzle, mists the air to keep humidity up, clears heat and CO2 with roof fans, and tells your phone when something is wrong.

The problem

Oyster mushrooms forgive very little. One missed day can end a flush.

Small growers keep their bags alive by hand. It works until the day nobody is home.

Key features

Five jobs the cabinet takes off your hands.

Heat kept down, with a 30 °C alarm

There is no compressor. When the cabinet warms up, the roof fans pull in fresh air and the spray cools it as it evaporates. If it stays above 30 °C for ten minutes, your phone gets an alert.

Mist first, then spray

When humidity drops, a mist maker sends fog along the ceiling and down over the bags. If the air is still dry after a set time, the row spray takes over.

A nozzle above every bag

Five spray pipes, one per shelf, carry a fine-mist nozzle over each of the 15 bags, so every bag is wetted directly.

Alerts on your phone

The app shows readings for the top, middle and bottom of the cabinet, with inside and outside shown separately. It alerts you when the cabinet runs too hot, the water tank runs low or a sensor goes offline.

Fresh air when CO2 builds up

A CO2 sensor watches the air inside. When CO2 or heat climbs, two exhaust fans on the roof run in short bursts and fresh air comes in through a vent low on the back wall.

How it's built

A cabinet, a backend and an app, working as one system.

An ESP32-S3 in the cabinet reads four temperature and humidity sensors (three zones inside, one outside), a CO2 sensor, a light sensor and the tank level. It drives the pump, spray valves, mist maker, roof fans and LED grow lights. It reports to a Spring Boot backend, and you watch the cabinet from the mobile app or a PC dashboard. GrowSync is built with the Mushroom Development and Training Centre (MDTC), and the demo cabinet is designed to scale to a full grow room later.

  1. ESP32-S3

    Reads the sensors and runs the control rules, in manual or auto mode. It keeps control if Wi-Fi drops.

  2. Spring Boot

    Stores readings and events, and raises alerts for high temperature, a low tank or an offline sensor.

  3. Mobile app and dashboard

    Live readings for each zone, history, thresholds and push alerts.
